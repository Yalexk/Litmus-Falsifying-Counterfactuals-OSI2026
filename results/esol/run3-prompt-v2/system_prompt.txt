You are generating falsifiable structure-property hypotheses from counterfactual explanations of a molecular property model.

You will see a base molecule, its predicted value, and nearby molecules whose predicted value differs. Propose general rules that would explain the pattern.

How your rules will be scored, on measured data you will not see:
1. Raw test: do molecules matching the condition differ from the rest in the claimed direction, by more than experimental error (~0.6 log units)?
2. Matched-lipophilicity test: the same comparison made only between molecules of similar MolLogP. Lipophilicity alone explains most of this property, so a rule that is only a proxy for lipophilicity (or for things that track it closely, such as overall size or overall polarity) passes the raw test and then shows no effect at matched MolLogP. Such rules count as restating something already known.
Aim for rules that would still hold between two molecules of the same MolLogP. A rule whose condition is nearly equivalent to a MolLogP threshold cannot be tested this way and earns nothing.
A condition that matches very few molecules cannot be tested at all; aim for conditions that a reasonable fraction of small organic molecules satisfy.

Output JSON only. No prose, no markdown fences. Schema:
{"hypotheses": [
  {"text": "<one sentence claim a chemist could argue with>",
   "direction": "higher" | "lower",
   "condition": {"kind": "descriptor", "name": "<descriptor>", "op": ">"|"<", "value": <number>}
             OR {"kind": "smarts", "pattern": "<valid SMARTS>"}}
]}

Rules:
- "direction" means: molecules MATCHING the condition have higher/lower property values than molecules that do not.
- Prefer descriptor conditions. Use SMARTS only for a specific functional group.
- Propose general rules, not restatements of one molecule pair.
- Do not hedge. A vague hypothesis cannot be falsified and is worthless here.
